# Engine architecture

`inphzugzwang-core` owns the board, move rules, FEN, hashing, and perft traversal. It has no external dependencies or I/O. `inphzugzwang-eval` computes a tapered middlegame and endgame score from material, piece-square tables, mobility, pawn structure, passed pawns, rook files, the bishop pair, king attackers and pawn shield, pieces attacked by lesser pieces, undefended attacked pieces, knight outposts, and passed-pawn king distance, with pawnless endgames scaled toward a draw when the stronger side has at most a minor piece extra. Every term reads a (middlegame, endgame) weight pair from `weights.rs` through a sink, so the tuner in `tools/tune` records exactly the features the engine sums. `inphzugzwang-nnue` bundles Stockfish 15.1's HalfKAv2_hm network `nn-ad9b42354671`, parses it at startup, and evaluates it with integer arithmetic that reproduces Stockfish 15.1 exactly. Each side's perspective sums 1,024 feature-transformer weights and eight piece-square values for every piece, kings included, relative to one of 32 buckets of its own king square, with the board mirrored so that the king stands on files e to h and rotated for Black. The two halves of each perspective are clipped to 0..127 and multiplied pairwise, side to move first, and the piece count picks one of eight layer stacks: a 1024-to-16 layer whose first fifteen outputs pass on both squared and clipped, a 30-to-32 layer and a 32-to-1 output, plus the sixteenth first-layer output directly and the piece-square difference. The affine layers use the dot-product instructions on aarch64 and AVX2 on x86-64 when the processor has them, and accumulator updates a second AVX2 build of the same loop, detected at runtime. A king move refreshes its side's accumulator from a per-thread cache holding the last accumulator for each king square, adding and removing only the pieces that differ. `inphzugzwang-syzygy` probes Syzygy WDL and DTZ tables, ported from Stockfish 13's `tbprobe`: tables are registered from the `.rtbw` files found, memory mapped on first use on Unix (read into memory elsewhere), and probed with Stockfish's indexing, Huffman and recursive-pairing decompression, and capture resolution. `inphzugzwang-search` owns iterative deepening, principal variation search, the transposition table, quiescence search, move ordering, and clock limits. `inphish` handles UCI, diagnostic commands, and bench output.

Squares use a1 = 0 through h8 = 63. The board combines piece-type bitboards, color bitboards, and a mailbox. A move stores origin, destination, and flag in 16 bits. Castling destinations in moves are the rook's starting square; the command formatter translates to king destinations for standard UCI notation.

Legal move generation computes checkers and pinned pieces for the side to move. Ordinary non-king moves obey the check mask and pin line. King moves and en passant account for the occupancy after the move. Castling verifies clear paths and king safety with the king and rook removed from their starting squares.

The attack table is initialized once. Rook and bishop lookups use verified magic multipliers on the portable path. On x86-64 with fast BMI2, they use a PEXT-indexed table. Zen 1 and Zen 2 use magics because their PEXT implementation is slow. Knight, king, pawn, between, and line data are precomputed at startup.

Position history is reserved before play and stores full states for exact unmake. Full, pawn, and per-side non-pawn keys use a fixed mixing seed. An en passant square contributes to the full key only when a legal capture exists. Debug builds recompute all keys after every make and unmake.

The UCI controller reads input on a dedicated thread and runs search on a separate large-stack thread. The controller alone writes to stdout. A stop flag interrupts the search; only the last fully completed iteration can replace the chosen move. The clock has soft and hard limits. A nearly exhausted clock is answered on the controller thread by a 1,000-node search, about a millisecond, so thread setup cannot consume the remaining time. With `Threads` above one the search is Lazy SMP: helper threads run their own iterative deepening on the same position, starting at depth 1 or 2 and with their own history tables, and share only the transposition table and a node counter. The main thread alone manages time, reports, and picks the move, and stops the helpers when it finishes.

The search keeps one network accumulator and one move list per ply. Each move derives the next accumulator from the previous by the features it removes and adds, and a king move refreshes its own side's perspective through the cache. The static evaluation takes the network's material-adjusted output, scales it by remaining material and damps it by the fifty-move counter as Stockfish 15.1 does without its optimism term, and converts it at 208 internal units per pawn. With `SyzygyPath` set and a root position of at most the tables' piece count and no castling rights, the root moves are ranked by DTZ as in Stockfish, only the best-ranked ones are searched, and the tablebase result is reported unless a mate is found. Otherwise the search probes WDL at nodes just after a capture or pawn move and returns decided wins, losses and draws, storing them in the transposition table. The search scores from the side-to-move perspective. From depth 5 each iteration starts with a 25-centipawn aspiration window around the previous score and doubles it on a fail, falling back to a full window. Checks extend by one ply. Windows are clipped to the mate distances reachable from the current ply. The transposition table is probed before any move is generated. Non-PV nodes use reverse futility pruning and null-move pruning when the side to move has a piece, both reading the stored score instead of the static evaluation where its bound makes it the tighter estimate, then ProbCut from depth 5: a capture that beats beta by 200 in quiescence and then at four plies less depth ends the node. Nodes without a stored move from depth 4 are searched a ply shallower. Near the leaves, late move pruning, futility pruning, history pruning and static-exchange pruning skip quiet moves, and static-exchange pruning skips captures that lose too much material. Late quiet moves are searched at a logarithmically reduced depth, two plies less at nodes expected to fail high, and re-searched when they beat alpha. Moves are ordered by the table move, captures that do not lose material by static exchange evaluation, two killers, a counter move for the previous move's piece and destination, butterfly history plus a one-ply continuation history keyed by the same previous move, and finally losing captures. A per-side pawn-structure correction history adds the average error that searches found for the current pawn structure to the static evaluation used for pruning and stand-pat. With `UCI_LimitStrength` on, the search caps its nodes at 200 for the lowest `UCI_Elo`, doubling every 240 Elo, searches at least four lines, and chooses among them the way Stockfish's skill level does: each line's score is raised in proportion to its gap from the best line plus a random share of the score spread, both scaled by a weakness that falls linearly to nothing at the top setting. With `MultiPV` above one, each later line searches the root moves not heading an earlier line once the first line of the depth completes. A single repetition inside the search is scored as a draw. Quiescence searches captures and promotions that do not lose material and whose victim could lift the stand-pat score within 200 centipawns of alpha, or all legal evasions while in check, and probes and stores the transposition table at depth zero. The transposition table uses four entries per 64-byte cluster. Its atomic key is XORed with the packed entry so an inconsistent concurrent read is unlikely to match the full position key. Entries persist across UCI searches and are replaced when the hash is resized or cleared. It reports mate distance, nodes, hash occupancy, elapsed time, and a principal variation. The deterministic bench searches 50 fixed positions at depth 4 with no clock limit and a fresh table per position.
