# Engine architecture

`inphzugzwang-core` owns the board, move rules, FEN, hashing, and perft traversal. It has no external dependencies or I/O. `inphzugzwang-eval` computes a tapered middlegame and endgame score from material, piece-square tables, mobility, pawn structure, passed pawns, rook files, the bishop pair, king attackers and pawn shield, pieces attacked by lesser pieces, undefended attacked pieces, knight outposts, and passed-pawn king distance, with pawnless endgames scaled toward a draw when the stronger side has at most a minor piece extra. Every term reads a (middlegame, endgame) weight pair from `weights.rs` through a sink, so the tuner in `tools/tune` records exactly the features the engine sums. `inphzugzwang-nnue` bundles Stockfish 19's network `nn-1a298aa575a0`, parses it at startup, and evaluates it with integer arithmetic that reproduces Stockfish 19 exactly. Each side's perspective sums 1,024 feature-transformer weights and eight piece-square values over three feature sets: HalfKAv2_hm pieces relative to one of 32 buckets of its own king square, threats of one piece on another (pawns on knights and rooks, knights and queens on every piece but kings, bishops and rooks on every piece but queens and kings), and pairs of pawns on the same or neighbouring files. Piece squares mirror the board so that the king stands on files e to h, threats and pawn pairs so that it stands on files a to d, and both rotate for Black. The two halves of each perspective are clipped to 0..255 and multiplied pairwise, side to move first, and the piece count picks one of eight layer stacks: a 1024-to-32 layer that reads only the groups of four inputs that are not zero, whose outputs pass on both squared and clipped to a 64-to-32 layer, whose outputs pass on the same way to a 128-to-1 output, plus the difference of the last two first-layer outputs and the piece-square difference. The affine layers use the dot-product instructions on aarch64 and AVX2 on x86-64 when the processor has them. As in Stockfish 19, making a move records what it changed: the pieces removed and placed, the pawns before and after, and every threat that appears or disappears, found while the pieces are lifted and put down (the threats of the piece itself, those it receives, and those of sliders through its square). An accumulator stack keeps one accumulator per move from the search's root, and a perspective is brought up to date only when a position is evaluated: forward from the last position where it was computed, each step adding and subtracting the changed features' rows in one tiled pass (Neon on aarch64, a second AVX2 build of the same loop on x86-64); after a move of its own king, by Stockfish's hybrid update when the king stays on its half of the board with at least 15 pieces left, or else by a refresh of the current position, from which the positions back to the king move are then derived backwards. A refresh takes the piece-square part from a per-thread cache holding the last accumulator for each king square, adding and removing only the pieces that differ, and sums the threats and pawn pairs afresh. The first layer finds its nonzero inputs with a bitmask of groups of four. As in Stockfish, the search loads the transposition-table entry of a move's position before making the move, from a key predicted from the move, and the new position's correction-history entries right after it. `inphzugzwang-book` computes Polyglot keys, hashing the en-passant file whenever a pawn of the side to move stands next to the pawn that moved two squares, pinned or not, as the format defines it, and finds a position's moves in a book of sorted 16-byte entries by binary search, matching them against the legal moves so that key collisions are ignored. It bundles inphish's own book, built by `tools/book` from Lichess games. `inphzugzwang-syzygy` probes Syzygy WDL and DTZ tables, ported from Stockfish 13's `tbprobe`: tables are registered from the `.rtbw` files found, memory mapped on first use on Unix (read into memory elsewhere), and probed with Stockfish's indexing, Huffman and recursive-pairing decompression, and capture resolution. `inphzugzwang-search` owns iterative deepening, principal variation search, the transposition table, quiescence search, move ordering, and clock limits. `inphish` handles UCI, diagnostic commands, and bench output.

Squares use a1 = 0 through h8 = 63. The board combines piece-type bitboards, color bitboards, and a mailbox. A move stores origin, destination, and flag in 16 bits. Castling destinations in moves are the rook's starting square; the command formatter translates to king destinations for standard UCI notation.

Each position stores its checkers and, for both kings, the pieces standing alone between the king and an enemy slider aimed at it: the side to move's own are pinned, and the opponent's can give discovered check, which lets the check test for a move work without playing it except for castling. Legal move generation uses the checkers and pins of the side to move. Ordinary non-king moves obey the check mask and pin line. King moves and en passant account for the occupancy after the move. Castling verifies clear paths and king safety with the king and rook removed from their starting squares.

The attack table is initialized once. Rook and bishop lookups use verified magic multipliers on the portable path. On x86-64 with fast BMI2, they use a PEXT-indexed table. Zen 1 and Zen 2 use magics because their PEXT implementation is slow. Knight, king, pawn, between, and line data are precomputed at startup.

Position history is reserved before play and stores full states for exact unmake. Full, pawn, minor-piece (knights and bishops) and per-side non-pawn keys use a fixed mixing seed. Each state stores, as Stockfish does, the distance to the last earlier occurrence of the position within the fifty-move counter, negative when that one was itself a repetition, and cuckoo tables built from the same keys find a reversible move that would repeat a position. An en passant square contributes to the full key only when a legal capture exists. Debug builds recompute all keys after every make and unmake.

The UCI controller reads input on a dedicated thread and runs search on a separate large-stack thread. The controller alone writes to stdout. A stop flag interrupts the search; only the last fully completed iteration can replace the chosen move. The clock has soft and hard limits. A nearly exhausted clock is answered on the controller thread by a 1,000-node search, about a millisecond, so thread setup cannot consume the remaining time. With `Threads` above one the search is Lazy SMP: helper threads run the same iterative deepening on the same position with their own histories, which they keep between moves, and share the transposition table, a node counter and the time manager's signals. The main thread manages time and reports; the move comes from Stockfish 19's vote among the threads, weighted by score, preferring the shortest proven mate.

The search is a port of Stockfish 19's and works in its internal units, 208 to a pawn, converting to centipawns only for reports. The static evaluation is Stockfish 19's: the network output shrunk where its two parts disagree, scaled with the material left, damped by the fifty-move counter, and leaned toward the side the root score favours by an optimism term, then corrected by pawn, minor-piece, non-pawn and continuation correction histories of the errors searches found. Iterative deepening searches each root move line in an aspiration window around the move's running average score, widened on a fail as Stockfish widens it. The node search follows Stockfish 19 step by step: mate distance pruning, a transposition-table cutoff at non-PV nodes checked one move ahead at depth 7 and more, tablebase probing, razoring, reverse futility pruning, null-move pruning at expected cut nodes with verification at depth 16 and more, internal iterative reductions, ProbCut, and in the move loop late move pruning, futility, history and static-exchange pruning, singular, double and triple extensions with multi-cut and negative extensions, and late move reductions in 1024ths of a ply adjusted by history, node type, the table entry and the correction size. Moves come from a staged picker: the table move, captures that do not lose too much by static exchange, ordered by capture history and victim value, quiet moves ordered by butterfly, low-ply, pawn and continuation histories with bonuses for safe checks and for leaving squares attacked by lesser pieces, then losing captures and the remaining quiet moves; evasions and quiescence captures have their own stages. After a node the histories are updated with Stockfish's bonuses and maluses. Quiescence searches captures and queen promotions that pass futility and static-exchange tests, or all evasions in check, standing pat on the corrected static evaluation. Time management is Stockfish 19's: an optimum and a maximum from the clock, with the optimum scaled after each iteration by how stable the best move is, how much of the effort went to it, and how the score fell, with a margin of 5 ms short of the flag. With `SyzygyPath` set and a root position of at most the tables' piece count and no castling rights, the root moves are ranked by DTZ as in Stockfish, only the best-ranked ones are searched, and the tablebase result is reported unless a mate is found; otherwise the search probes WDL just after captures and pawn moves. With `UCI_LimitStrength` on, the search caps its nodes by a budget interpolated between calibration points, searches at least four lines, and chooses among them the way Stockfish's skill level does, with a weakness that falls linearly to nothing at 2600; above 2600 only the node budget limits play. With `MultiPV` above one, each later line searches the root moves not heading an earlier line. The transposition table holds four entries per 64-byte cluster, each with a key XORed with its packed data so an inconsistent concurrent read is unlikely to match, and follows Stockfish 19's replacement and aging rules; its key changes every eight plies once the fifty-move counter reaches 14. The deterministic bench searches 50 fixed positions at depth 13 with no clock limit and a fresh table per position.
